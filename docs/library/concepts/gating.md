You've probably made one already: a door you can't find until you have the
lantern, a shop that opens after the festival, a skill you learn only after
talking to someone.

That's **gating**. Something is behind a gate, and the gate opens when a
condition is met.

* * *

Designers use gates to pace a story (not everything at once), to make an
achievement feel earned, and to teach one thing before the next thing that
needs it.

A gate works best when the player can tell there *is* something behind it,
even before they can get in.

* * *

## Words to know

- **Gating:** access depends on a condition.
- **Progression gate:** a gate that controls how far along the player can get.
- **Soft gate:** you *can* get past early, but it's hard or risky.
- **Hard gate:** you can't get past until the condition is met.

* * *

## Under the hood

Usually a check on a remembered fact (a *flag*): "has the lantern" → show
the door. In VEFR, an earned Library book (`found: earned`) stays hidden
until its moment happens: the same idea.
